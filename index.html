PLOS Computational Biology · 2026 · Second of five authors

Latent encoding of movement in primary visual cortex

H. Germaine, I. Choi, S. D. Van Hooser, J. Ribot, J. D. Touboul · Brandeis University and Collège de France (CNRS)

The textbook picture is that visual cortex lays out each stimulus feature as a preference map, but for temporal frequency and speed that map is nearly flat. I built and ran the CNN decoding analyses: with 10-fold cross-validation against shuffled-label controls, temporal frequency, direction, and speed were all decodable from optical imaging of cat visual cortex, even from patches about 0.12 mm across. The information is there, just not in the map.

Independent research · Dartmouth College · 2026 to present

Overnight FX and the Tokyo opening auction

Japanese metal miners sell in dollars and pay costs in yen, so a weaker yen should lift their shares. Does Tokyo's opening auction fully price the overnight USD/JPY move, or does the stock keep drifting after the open? An earlier version of the question, predicting the opening gap itself, found an in-sample link that failed out of sample, and the gap isn't tradable anyway. So I reframed it around open-to-close returns and pre-registered the test: hypotheses and a power analysis locked on 2015 to 2021 data, with 2022 to 2026 data hashed and sealed until then. The effect was significant in the development years and did not replicate in the sealed sample; most of what remained came from the August 2024 yen carry unwind. I reported the null.

Sumitomo Metal Mining, Mitsubishi Materials, Mitsui Kinzoku · Blinded pipeline with 398 tests, built through a test-gated Claude Code workflow

Next: the same locked-test design in emerging markets, where opening auctions may price information less efficiently.

ENGS 104 Optimization Methods · Thayer School of Engineering · Fall 2026

The Shadow Price of Neutrality

Portfolio platforms often read an optimizer's dual variables as what each risk rule costs a pod. That's exact for convex problems and stops being exact for integer ones. I priced every constraint on a $1B long/short S&P 500 book (momentum alpha, six-factor risk model, neutrality, sector, gross, and liquidity limits) across 12 dates from 2008 to 2025. Liquidity was the expensive rule: halving the cap cost about a quarter of predicted alpha, while requiring zero style-factor exposure cost about 4%. I then added integer rules (minimum position sizes, name caps), graded three cheap cost estimators against certified true costs, and checked when the solver's own bounds and certificates could be trusted.
